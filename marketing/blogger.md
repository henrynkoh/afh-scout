## How to Screen a Washington Home for an Adult Family Home Conversion

A listing price alone cannot tell you whether a house works as an AFH investment. The decision depends on financing, six months of holding costs, remodel scope, garage capacity, rent after conversion, county taxes, HOA, and the licensing and inspection path.

AFH Scout makes that process repeatable. Its entry is a Zillow property link. The app verifies the public listing, filters the house against an AFH buy box, and then runs a 5 × 5 matrix: purchase prices from $450,000 to $550,000 and rents from $3,500 to $6,000.

Every scenario includes $20,000 of six-month holding cost, $60,000 for remodeling, and $10,000 of contingency. The table reports monthly cash flow, NPV, IRR, operational payback, five-year ROI, and a 1-10 judgement with the weakest metric named.

The second half of the decision is physical. AFH Scout flags garage size, septic capacity, egress windows, slab and header review, WSEC energy work, smoke and CO alarms, HVAC, parking, zoning, and DSHS whole-home licensing review. These are not approvals, but they form a controlled due-diligence checklist before an offer.

The most useful output is not “yes” or “no.” It is the boundary: the maximum purchase price at a given rent, or the minimum rent at the asking price, required to clear the investment score.
